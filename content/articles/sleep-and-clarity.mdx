## Sleep is when your brain cleans itself

During sleep — particularly deep (slow-wave) sleep — the brain's glymphatic system activates. This is a waste-clearance system that flushes metabolic byproducts, including amyloid-beta and tau proteins associated with neurodegeneration. A single night of poor sleep measurably increases these waste products.

Memory consolidation also happens during sleep. The hippocampus replays experiences and transfers them to long-term storage. Without adequate sleep, this process is incomplete, which is why learning and recall feel impaired after a poor night.

## How much sleep do you actually need?

The research consensus for adults is 7–9 hours, with individual variation. The key insight is that sleep debt is real and cumulative: sleeping 6 hours for five nights creates a deficit equivalent to pulling an all-nighter. Catching up on weekends partially (but not fully) reverses this.

<Callout type="info">
Studies show that people who chronically sleep less than 7 hours often don't feel subjectively impaired — their brain adapts to feeling foggy as a baseline. Objective cognitive performance, however, remains significantly reduced.
</Callout>

## Sleep quality vs. sleep quantity

Total hours matter, but so does architecture — the ratio of light, deep, and REM sleep. Alcohol, for example, may help you fall asleep faster but dramatically suppresses REM sleep, leaving you feeling foggy even after 8 hours.

## Practical sleep hygiene

- Keep a consistent wake time — even on weekends (this is more powerful than bedtime)
- Keep your bedroom cool (18–19°C / 65–67°F is optimal for most people)
- Avoid bright light and screens 60–90 minutes before bed
- No alcohol within 3 hours of sleep
- Avoid caffeine after 2pm (half-life is 5–7 hours)
- Reserve your bed for sleep — not work, scrolling, or TV

<Callout type="tip">
If you track sleep in the FoggyBrain app, focus on consistency first. Variability in sleep timing — even with adequate hours — is independently associated with worse cognitive outcomes.
</Callout>
